⭐ Piazza del Duomo, Crema
🎬 In the film: the café terrace where Oliver asks "So… what do people do around here?", the newsagent La Provincia (at no. 14, now closed) that Elio watches Oliver leave, where Elio meets Marzia — and the start and end of the bicycle rides.
🏛 History: the first cathedral here was razed by Emperor Frederick Barbarossa after the brutal seven-month Siege of Crema (1159–60), when the whole town was destroyed. The Duomo you see was rebuilt 1284–1340 in Lombard Gothic — brick, rose window, soaring bell tower.
📍 ⭐ Piazza del Duomo, Crema
Arco del Torrazzo (cathedral arch)
🎬 In the film: the Renaissance archway looming beside Elio and Oliver’s café table — the backdrop of the piazza scenes.
🏛 History: built in 1525, a gift to the city from Francesco II Sforza, Duke of Milan. Walk through it — it links the piazza to the old market square behind.
📍 Arco del Torrazzo (cathedral arch)
Porta Serio (town gate)
🎬 In the film: the old gate the bikes roll through in the cycling sequences.
🏛 History: one of Crema’s two surviving town gates, on the east road toward the Serio river. Crema spent 350 years (1449–1797) as the westernmost outpost of the Republic of Venice — a fortified island of Venetian territory inside Milanese Lombardy, which is why the old town feels subtly different from its neighbours.
📍 Porta Serio (town gate)

🍬 About those tortelli cremaschi: Crema’s signature pasta is stuffed with amaretti, candied citron, raisins and spices — a sweet-savoury combination that is a direct legacy of the Venetian spice trade that ran through the town for centuries. Nobody else in Italy makes them like this.
